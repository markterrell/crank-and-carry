Buying guide 01

How to choose a wind-up radio for power cuts

A portable emergency radio can help you keep up with local news and official updates when mains power or mobile services are disrupted. The best choice depends on your needs, local reception and how you plan to keep it charged.

Quick advice: prioritise dependable FM/DAB reception where available, a clearly specified battery and a practical way to recharge it. Treat hand-crank and solar charging as backup methods, not guaranteed replacements for keeping the radio charged.

Check reception first

FM is widely used and often straightforward to tune. DAB or DAB+ offers digital stations where coverage exists, but reception varies by location. Check the stations and coverage available where you live before paying extra for digital features.

Understand power options

  • Replaceable batteries: useful for storing spares; check type and runtime.
  • Built-in rechargeable battery: convenient, but check charging method and whether it is replaceable.
  • Hand crank: useful backup, but can require sustained effort for limited power.
  • Solar panel: may help in bright sunlight; small panels can charge slowly, especially indoors or in winter.
  • USB charging: convenient if you have a charged power bank.

Which extras are worth it?

A torch, reading light, phone-charging output or alarm may help, but extras can add bulk and complexity. Check actual charging output and battery capacity; do not assume the radio can fully recharge a smartphone.

Before you buy

  • Check size, weight and ease of use.
  • Look for stated capacity, charging time and runtime.
  • Confirm what is included in the box.
  • Read recent reviews, warranty and returns information.

Keep your radio ready

Follow the manufacturer's instructions, charge it before storing, test reception and check its charge periodically. Store it somewhere accessible. A radio is one part of preparedness, alongside a torch, water, essential medicines and a way to charge your phone.

Buying guide 02

How to choose a battery-powered radio for camping

A battery radio is a simple companion for a campsite, caravan, garden or trip where you do not want to rely on a mains socket. Focus on battery life, reception and portability before paying for extras.

Quick checklist: confirm battery type, check stated runtime, test reception where possible, and consider a protective case if the radio will travel in a packed bag.

Choose the right battery setup

Compact radios may use AA or AAA batteries; larger models may use C cells or a built-in rechargeable pack. Replaceable batteries make it easy to carry spares, while rechargeable models can be economical if you have a reliable way to recharge them. Check whether batteries are included and whether runtime figures are published.

FM or DAB?

FM is often simple and can be power-efficient, depending on the model. DAB/DAB+ provides digital stations where coverage is available, but may use more power and can be less useful where digital reception is weak. Check coverage at your destination if you want DAB.

Portability and durability

  • Choose a size and weight you are happy to carry.
  • Look for controls that are easy to use outdoors.
  • Check any stated splash resistance; do not assume a radio is waterproof.
  • Consider a handle, carry strap or protective case.
  • Check display readability in daylight and low light.

Useful extras

A headphone socket can be useful for quiet listening. Presets make stations quicker to find. Bluetooth may be handy if you want a speaker too, but can add cost and power use. A built-in torch is convenient but should not replace a proper camping light.

Before you leave home

Fit fresh or fully charged batteries, test the stations you want, pack spares if appropriate, and keep the radio dry. Hills, buildings and distance can affect signal strength.

Buying guide 03

DAB vs FM: which portable radio should you choose?

Portable radios often list FM, DAB or DAB+ in their specifications. Each has strengths. Your choice should depend on the stations available where you live or travel, power needs and how simple you want the radio to be.

FM in brief

FM can be a good choice if you want a simple, affordable radio, listen to familiar stations or need a basic backup. As signal weakens, FM may become noisy.

DAB and DAB+ in brief

DAB/DAB+ can offer a wider selection of stations where digital coverage is available, and station selection can be straightforward. Reception can sound clear until the signal drops out. Not every station is available everywhere, so check coverage before buying.

What about battery life?

Battery performance depends on the radio's design, screen, speaker volume, battery type and reception conditions. Compare manufacturer figures and instructions. A dual FM/DAB model gives flexibility, but check how easily it switches between modes.

Five checks before buying

  1. Which stations you want and which formats carry them locally.
  2. Whether it uses replaceable batteries, a rechargeable pack or mains power.
  3. How easy it is to tune and save presets.
  4. Whether it has a headphone socket or other features you need.
  5. Its size, weight, warranty and return terms.

Our verdict: a radio supporting both FM and DAB/DAB+ can offer flexibility. If you prefer simplicity and lower cost, FM may be enough. Coverage and battery life matter more than choosing a format based on its label alone.

Good to know

Frequently asked questions

Do wind-up radios work without mains electricity?

Many have a hand crank and/or internal rechargeable battery, but features vary. Check the exact model instructions and do not assume every model has solar charging or phone charging.

Is DAB reception available everywhere?

No. Reception depends on location, buildings and available stations. Check coverage and the retailer's return policy before choosing a DAB-only radio.
